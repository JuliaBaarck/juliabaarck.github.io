About

I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the ifo Institute and LMU Munich, where I am part of the ifo Center for Public Economics. I expect to complete my PhD in 2027; my advisor is Andreas Peichl.

My research is in labor and public economics. I study how institutions affect the careers people end up in, how inequality is transmitted across generations, and what shapes people’s views about fairness and redistribution. I work primarily with large-scale administrative and survey data.

Before starting my PhD I earned an M.Sc. in Econometrics and Empirical Economics from the Toulouse School of Economics and a B.Sc. in Economics from the University of Mannheim. During my PhD I have visited the University of California, San Diego and the Aix-Marseille School of Economics.

Job market paper

Try Before You Choose: Early Work Experience and Occupational Choice

Occupations shape individuals’ economic and non-economic outcomes, yet young people often choose a field with little knowledge of the work. Can a short work experience, assigned at the moment they choose a career, change what they do for a living? Using a difference-in-discontinuities design, I exploit that the suspension of German conscription in 2011 split one cohort of high-track graduates at the school-entry cutoff: men born before the cutoff could still be drafted into the military or the social alternative service, men born after could not.

The draft shifts young men towards the occupations of the service, and persists through their late twenties. The shift is selective, moving individuals towards the service-related occupations with better working conditions and more qualified positions, and comes at no detectable cost to education, labor market entry and earnings. Surveys of former participants point to information as a channel. An early work experience, assigned at a formative moment, can thus shape occupational choice, even for those who would not have chosen it.
